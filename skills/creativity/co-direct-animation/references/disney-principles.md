# Disney animation principles

Sources: [Dylan Tarre's collection](https://github.com/dylantarre/animation-principles/tree/83597134ba8ff59838270f94d7ac7282ffa3b54d), specifically [video motion graphics](https://github.com/dylantarre/animation-principles/blob/83597134ba8ff59838270f94d7ac7282ffa3b54d/skills/01-by-domain/video-motion-graphics/SKILL.md) and [creative direction](https://github.com/dylantarre/animation-principles/blob/83597134ba8ff59838270f94d7ac7282ffa3b54d/skills/03-by-role-persona/creative-director/SKILL.md), inspected 2026-10-11. Community adaptations of Johnston and Thomas's principles; not an official Disney skill or one fixed visual style. Evidence level: documented practice.

Use the twelve principles as questions: deformation for weight, anticipation, staging, straight-ahead versus planned poses, follow-through and overlap, acceleration, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, spatial solidity and appeal. Select only what helps the scene. They are a vocabulary, not twelve mandatory effects.

Stage one main action; subordinate details to it. Let timing convey intention. A secondary element can settle after the primary one. Keep volume and identity coherent across generated frames.

Pull toward the author: never deform a protected logo, add characters, turn a restrained identity into a cartoon, or impose overshoot where the source forbids it. Put expressive motion in compatible supporting elements instead. Use literal motion when it serves the author better than an arc.
