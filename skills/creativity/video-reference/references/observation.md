# Observe behavior, preserve evidence

First inspect available visual/audio capabilities. Use the original and timestamped evidence to cover the entire source. If listening or continuous video inspection is unavailable, say which claims remain unverified. Do not fill missing observation with plausible descriptions.

Pass one captures the complete arc, cuts, persistent systems, spoken/on-screen content, audio roles and uncertainties. Pass two deepens events where timing or construction matters. Fast transitions, word reveals, chart assembly and sound attacks need more than a sparse contact sheet. Existing local ASR/OCR/beat tools may help, but label their output and verify critical details.

Use the schema's seven layers. For each event record source interval, observed/inferred basis, evidence IDs and details. Empty layers do not imply confirmed absence: describe the basis for absence or the missing capability. Coverage is your attestation, not a score generated by the CLI.

| Layer | Describe concretely |
|---|---|
| Content | Wording, meaning, entities and argument; uncertain transcript segments |
| Structure | Hook, development, contrast, reveal/payoff and each pause's role |
| Visual | Framing, proportions, occlusion, cuts, composition and continuity |
| Text | Copy, word groups/line breaks, hierarchy, position, entrance, emphasis, hold, exit |
| Graphics | Parts, visible data, order of construction, accumulation and transformations |
| Motion | Start/end states, trajectory, acceleration/deceleration, timing and overlap |
| Audio | Speech, music, onset/beat distinction, effects, silence, tails, levels and purpose |

A useful description: “The number appears first; the unit fades in later; three bars grow left to right, each completing at its corresponding word.” Add evidence and measured timing when available. “Dynamic chart with animated text” cannot guide reconstruction.

Represent shared timing and meaning through event relations. A sound may emphasize a word, accompany an object's entry, or follow music independently. Distinguish coincidence from inferred intent. Separate the original clock from a suggested adaptation rule. A guessed easing curve or font is a reconstruction hypothesis.

Save provenance for accepted upstream transcript/source revisions when available. Do not infer acceptance from repository presence or a stale checkout. Use local source identity and the connector's accepted references; do not query unrelated batches. If corrected text invalidated word alignment, leave timing unknown or measure it again.

Unknowns include unidentifiable fonts, illegible numbers, overlapping words, ambiguous sound sources, approximate trajectories and unsupported causal claims. Visual appearance cannot establish virality; performance metrics require their own source and date.
