# Editorial triptych

The default campaign format: three panels stacked into one image, one photographer's eye, one muse.

## Look

A cohesive three-panel editorial layout, stacked top, middle, bottom, framed tightly throughout. The photographic voice is that of a world-class fashion photographer. Name the tradition (cinematic editorial close-up portraiture) and use a living photographer's name only when the user asks for it.

| Panel | Framing | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Top | Tight portrait on eyes and face | Intense gaze, visible skin texture, emotion |
| Middle | Tight crop of one body part in motion or rest | Hands holding something, a neck, a posture: attitude without the full body |
| Bottom | Extreme macro | A garment's weave, a piece of jewelry, a prop that belongs to the brand's life, or a texture from its world |

## Prompt blocks

- **Muse.** The casting line from `brief.md`: the same person in the top and middle panels.
- **Styling.** Avant-garde, tactile, expensive-looking, with one deliberate oddity the brand could own (an unexpected accessory, a juxtaposed garment, a prop).
- **Technical look.** Hyper-real: strong film grain, pores and fabric weave, realistic depth of field. Light chosen from the DNA brief: chiaroscuro, colored gels, harsh sun or moody shadow.
- **Grade.** A sophisticated editorial palette taken from the DNA palette line.

## Inspect

- Three panels, stacked, in the intended order and framing.
- The muse in the top and middle panels is the same person and matches the casting line.
- Palette and light match the DNA brief; nothing on the Never line appears.
- Every logo, wordmark or printed text on garments and props matches the real mark or is flagged as a defect; the model invents marks.
- Hands, jewelry and fabric are anatomically and physically plausible.
- The oddity reads as intentional and brand-owned, not as noise.
