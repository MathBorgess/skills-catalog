# Mindful Movement: objects as people

Origin: the creative guideline supplied during this skill's design, 2026-10-12. This is the workflow's core directing method, not a claim of independently measured results. Load it for every work, including works with a custom reference registry. The original DESIGN.md wins over references; an explicit current author instruction may override a specific restriction when recorded for the work.

## Cast objects, then choreograph relationships

Treat an object as a performer with a purpose, not a vector waiting for a transform. Ask what it wants, what it notices, how it prepares and what the movement makes the viewer understand. Physical awareness must be visible in its timing and interaction with other objects.

For each focal or interacting object record:

| Property | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Intention | A verb and its target: approach, invite, inspect, yield, challenge, embrace, depart. |
| Emotional core | The mood and the movement's origin. A chest-led upper pivot can feel confident or curious; a hip-led lower pivot can feel grounded or heavy. These are expressive proposals, not universal psychological facts. Specify the actual transform origin. |
| Physical character | Relative weight, gravity direction, friction/resistance, spring response and damping. State what changes when the object meets a surface or another performer. |
| Elasticity | Which parts can deform, which stay rigid, and the amplitude/angle/scale bounds allowed by the original. Preserve volume or projected area when that supports the intended material. |
| Personal bubble | A visible object contour plus intentional clearance. Name the objects/boundaries it respects, and whether contact, overlap, wrapping or collision is intentional. |
| Readability | The settled pose and reading window. Keep body copy stable; transform display type only when meaning and recovery remain legible. |

Objects without a motion role may hold still intentionally. Rest, suspension and a clear final pose are part of the choreography. Do not animate every object independently just to fill the screen.

## Write a causal motion score

Plan meaningful phases with times and relationships:

1. **Notice and anticipate.** Shift weight, tilt, compress, turn attention or draw slightly backward before committing. A protected rigid asset can prepare by changing its trajectory or a surrounding cue instead of deforming.
2. **Push and travel.** Move from a chosen origin. Let acceleration, arcs and stretch communicate propulsion and weight. Carry position and velocity across phases rather than restarting each movement from rest.
3. **Negotiate space.** Slow, lean, pivot, yield or bend around the other performer's bubble. For UI, preserve hit targets and do not make the user wait for ornamental anticipation before feedback.
4. **Meet and transfer energy.** Proximity can unfold a card; contact can compress it; an impact can release a letter group. The partner reacts to the encounter, not an unrelated timer. Intentional overlap belongs in the score; unexplained clipping is a defect.
5. **Recover and depart.** Let secondary parts lag, recoil or settle. Restore readable type and coherent silhouettes. Exit together only when the relationship calls for it; preserve author-defined entry/exit logic.

For code, derive a continuous, seekable timeline; avoid frame-dependent randomness. Spring response/damping, mass and friction are directing parameters until a renderer maps them to a specific simulation. For image/video models, describe the same phases, contact, pivots and shape invariants in shot prompts. A name such as “mindful” is insufficient on its own.

## Transformation vocabulary

Use varied transformations where they express different intentions. There is no effect quota; repeat a signature purposefully and give important objects more than a translation/opacity treatment when the author permits it.

| Family | How it expresses life | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Arc, orbit, detour, lateral weight shift | Curiosity, respect, attraction, avoidance | Plan a path around real object bounds. |
| Lean, counter-rotation, pivot | Looking, offering a side, confidence, groundedness | Record the pivot; keep protected orientation unchanged. |
| Squash, stretch, recoil, overshoot | Loading effort, landing, softness, stored energy | Use complementary scales when preserving 2D area; use volume-aware deformation in 3D. Bound the spring and settle it. |
| Skew, bend, twist, curl, fold/unfold | Reaching, tension, revealing, embracing | Deform only approved shapes/parts; avoid illegible text and logo damage. |
| Ripple, delayed segments, overlap | Energy traveling through a material or group | Follow a cause, with secondary motion after the primary. |
| Split, scatter, regroup, morph | Impact, surprise, transformation of an idea | Use editable vectors/glyphs, preserve identity, and rebuild a readable pose. Never invent intermediate brand marks. |
| Percussive stop, stutter, damped vibration, rhythmic scale | Friction, impact, punk/funk groove | Tie it to a beat or collision; bound displacement, keep brightness stable and provide reduced motion. |

Elasticity is a default for deformable performers. If the author explicitly permits logo transformation, give it the same expressive range as other performers within the recorded scope: compress, stretch, lean, bend or morph as appropriate, then recover its recognizable identity. If the original protects it and no current instruction changes that, move it as a rigid body and give its permitted supporting shapes the bend, squash or ripple. Record the permission or adaptation explicitly.

## Two choreographic directions

**Calm romantic duet — sustained and suspended.** A logo-like performer notices a card group, prepares and approaches on a wide arc. It decelerates before the group's bubble and suspends nearby; a permitted lean expresses attention. The cards unfold from their centers in response to proximity and trail the leader's timing. The leader wraps around their space. Both depart along compatible paths with a shared slow release. Use long phrasing and held moments, not perpetual floating unrelated to the encounter.

**Punk/funk exchange — percussive and vibratory.** A bold performer loads a short anticipation, accelerates hard and meets the typography's declared contact surface. Braking and deformation communicate resistance; overshoot/recoil expresses spring recovery rather than being described as friction itself. The word separates into glyphs which receive the impact, scatter within a bounded region and catch the groove. Small rhythmic stutters and pulses lead them back to their original readable grid with sharp timing. Do not shake the entire camera or flash the screen to substitute for object interaction.

These directions are examples, not mandatory genres. Preserve the original tone when either would contradict it. Contact can be replaced by a respectful near-encounter; deformation can move to a permitted companion shape.

## Inspect the relationship, not just the endpoints

Inspect anticipation, peak deformation, nearest approach, contact, recoil and the readable recovery. Check transformed contours and swept space, not only undeformed boxes. Verify that the reacting object's movement follows its cause, protected assets remain intact and no glyph vanishes permanently. Test the full timeline for excessive jitter and clipping; demonstrate reduced motion with stable poses/crossfades while retaining the narrative. Record the actual observations and any deviations in delivery.json. No check here establishes user approval.
