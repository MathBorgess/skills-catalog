# Storyboard, milestones and speech cleanup

Use this method when production is requested. Current project MANDATES and explicit author choices prevail. Carry the requirements into REVIEW.md and the editor handoff. The same process should work with another speaker, brand, subject, reference or destination; concrete fonts, colors, glow strength, card dimensions and keywords are project choices.

## Build and maintain the storyboard

Before assembly, map the whole intended arc with timestamped prints and source speech. Update the board after cuts and during Studio review. Cover every chapter, important milestone, CTA and cut candidate; combine repeated shots where useful rather than force one panel per second. The second-by-second REVIEW remains required separately.

For each panel record:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Identity and clocks | Panel/chapter ID, source interval, destination interval/frame and print timestamp |
| Print and provenance | Link to a real source, reference or draft screenshot; identify its revision and role. Label illustrations and historical drafts. Record missing evidence instead of presenting a placeholder as a capture. |
| Speech | Actual excerpt and basis: direct listening, model hypothesis or author correction. Mark uncertain words; record explicit absence of speech when applicable. |
| Editorial action | Keep, cut, shorten or emphasize, with purpose and reason; proposed changes remain distinct from changes applied to the timeline. |
| Assembly | Text hierarchy, graphics construction, entrance/hold/exit, image and audio anchors, dependent layers and editable file/prop |
| Review | Inspected interval/frames, observation method, issue and pass/fail/unverified per relevant dimension |

Preserve originals and existing evidence. Capture through a color-compatible path; if a capture operation cannot preserve source color, describe that limitation before running it and obey project mandates. Prints help editorial review but cannot establish continuous motion, audible continuity or HDR fidelity. Keep private prints outside public source repositories.

## Give milestones and CTA greater weight

Identify the actual hook, discovery, attempt, setback, contrast, result and closing invitation in the content. Explain why each selected milestone matters. Give the important moments visibly greater weight than ordinary speech using type size/weight, contrast, scale, motion, graphic construction and readable holds. Reduce competing labels. Anchor entrances to the author's actual words; an authentic admission of failure may be central to the story and must not be erased as a delivery mistake.

For the CTA, make the intended action and keyword unmistakable and give them room to be read. Use the author's existing or explicitly authorized invitation, without adding promises or unsupported results. If no CTA fits the intent, record absent/not applicable and propose copy separately. Brand-colored glow may suit one reference; choose the effect from the current identity and keep it on graphics, preserving source-video color. Do not transfer a particular keyword, font, pixel size or presenter crop as a universal default.

## Inspect every speech-editing opportunity

Review the complete spoken source for delivery mistakes, duplicate takes, false starts, redundant repetition and idle pauses. Use available local transcription/alignment as support when helpful; this requires inspection, not an installed ASR model or a separate transcript-approval stage.

Keep a ledger containing candidate ID, source interval, before/removed/after excerpts, destination join, proposed/applied/retained state, reason, evidence and affected layers. Apply reversible cuts when they improve pace and preserve meaning and continuity. Retain intentional repetition, useful pauses, qualifiers, uncertainty and relevant admissions of failure. A phrase beginning with an error word is not automatically disposable. No fixed cut rate, duration target or compulsory removal applies.

Inspect both sides of each join: word completeness, breath/tail, acoustic discontinuity, face/gesture continuity, graphic state and caption timing. Remap native footage, original audio, captions, B-roll, graphics, motion and matte together; a split shot should preserve its animation state. If wording or meaning is ambiguous, retain it or present the material decision rather than guess. Inspect candidate intervals and record retained decisions even when no cut is safe.

## Verify the resulting video

Review the complete composition in actual Studio, then deepen critical poses, CTA, milestones and joins. Protect faces and destination overlays. For foreground occlusion, compare framing/clocks with the original and inspect enlarged hair, glasses, jaw, shoulders, hands and consecutive motion frames. A rectangular face patch cannot establish a good matte. Technique and sampling depend on the shot rather than one fixed model or resolution.

Report structure, visual, audio, color and human acceptance separately as pass/fail/unverified with the inspected scope, actionable findings and editing controls. File counts, source hashes, schema tests and server startup do not establish audiovisual fidelity. A storyboard supports a full editable first pass; it does not replace it or add an approval round. Export/publication still require a specific request.
