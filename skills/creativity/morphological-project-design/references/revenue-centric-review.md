# Revenue-centric candidate review

Adapted from **Richard ([@richardrx](https://x.com/richardrx))** through the [pinned Revenue-Centric Design source](https://github.com/heliocosta-dev/revenue-centric-design/tree/1942760c2242f609bf63ce2c97204f5a1791bd68). Read [provenance and scope](../PROVENANCE.md); the complete [specific reuse terms](../RCD-LICENSE.txt) apply. Do not apply this material to the excluded gambling, betting, casino or real-money games-of-chance uses.

## Fix the comparison before generating candidates

Record the criteria, weights, evidence labels and rejection conditions before reading proposals. Cover the payer and costly problem, economic plausibility, smallest useful scope, reachable channel and proposed difference. Keep the same criteria across both rounds; disclose any later correction and its effect instead of adjusting weights to favor a candidate. Scores are judgments, never probabilities of success. A high total cannot erase a fatal condition.

## Proponent: explain the proposed exchange

For every candidate, identify:

1. **Buyer and user.** Name a narrow buyer, budget source, triggering situation and important loss or burden. Distinguish the person using the output from the person paying.
2. **Outcome and existing alternative.** Explain the useful result, the current workflow and why switching might be worthwhile. Include manual work and general-purpose tools; missing information about a competitor is not an absent feature.
3. **Offer and channel.** Specify a hypothetical charging unit, price, usage frequency and plausible route to the first qualified buyers. Existing access is evidence only when supplied or verified. Do not call the founder's acquisition time free.
4. **Scope and path.** Interpret every sampled choice without replacing it. Explain tensions and the smallest useful deliverable. State which capabilities and integrations are planned, available or unverified.
5. **Economics and next test.** State assumptions, costs, sensitivity and the evidence that would change the recommendation.

The relevant source principles are [a deliberate ICP and reachable market](https://github.com/heliocosta-dev/revenue-centric-design/blob/1942760c2242f609bf63ce2c97204f5a1791bd68/references/positioning-icp-and-gtm.md) and [pricing between cost and buyer value](https://github.com/heliocosta-dev/revenue-centric-design/blob/1942760c2242f609bf63ce2c97204f5a1791bd68/references/pricing-and-monetization.md). Adapt them to the candidate; do not import the source's example prices, conversion rates or market sizes.

## Critic: stress the economics and the reason to buy

Review every candidate and every path choice. Separate these quantities:

| Quantity | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | Price times paid volume over a declared period, with currency and gross/net basis. Proposed sales remain hypothetical. |
| Cash contribution | Revenue less the included cash costs attributable to delivery and acquisition. List exactly what is included; this is not total business profit. |
| Economic contribution | Cash contribution less labor valued but not already paid in the cash costs, and less any additional explicitly allocated costs. Do not count the same labor or acquisition cost twice. |
| Buyer benefit | Incremental benefit over the current alternative, less purchase, adoption and switching costs. It is distinct from the seller's revenue. |

Account for model usage, infrastructure, payment charges, refunds, delivery, support, revision effort and acquisition. Show acquisition spend and acquisition labor separately and allocate them over a stated number of buyers and period. Price labor using an explicit rate and time estimate. Include applicable taxes when supported; otherwise mark their treatment unknown and label subtotals accordingly. Unknown costs do not become zero. Keep fixed development and overhead separate unless the allocation is explicit.

Stress the inputs that could reverse the conclusion: fewer paying buyers, more support, repeated generations, higher acquisition effort or lower repeat use. If contribution is positive, a fixed-cost recovery estimate is fixed cost divided by comparable contribution, rounded up; if it is non-positive, that recovery claim is unsupported. Subscription calculations require a stated time horizon and retention assumptions; do not assume indefinite payments.

For the buyer, time saved is not automatically cash saved. State whether time can be redeployed or expenditure avoided, and what would demonstrate that benefit. If computing a return ratio, declare the baseline, period and complete buyer outlay. Leave the return unknown when those inputs are missing.

Give the strongest objection, weakest assumption, revisions requested and comparative rationale. Permit rejection of every candidate. A recommendation for a future paid test is not investment approval or commercial validation. During the revision round, each proponent answers its own feedback while preserving the path; the critic then compares all N revised candidates. After dedicated competitor research, the critic may withdraw or reselect among those revisions; research a replacement before accepting it, without another generation round.

## Define a future test without claiming it happened

Specify the buyer cohort, offer and price, observable action, time window, success/rejection conditions and costs to collect. Include delivery completion and buyer outcome alongside willingness to pay. Track paid conversion, actual operational effort and contribution separately from interviews, signups or enthusiastic comments. Record missing observations as unknown.

Use the source's [signal-quality and experimentation guidance](https://github.com/heliocosta-dev/revenue-centric-design/blob/1942760c2242f609bf63ce2c97204f5a1791bd68/references/metrics-and-experimentation.md) to avoid treating a small exploratory test as statistically conclusive. Positive interest is weaker evidence than a completed paid exchange; even payment alone does not establish repeat demand, buyer return or scalable profit. Planning a test does not authorize contacting buyers, accepting money or buying services.
